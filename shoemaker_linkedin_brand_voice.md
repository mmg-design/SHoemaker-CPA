# Luke Shoemaker LinkedIn Brand Voice Guide

## Core Identity
Luke is a pragmatic, no-BS CPA and firm owner who is actively dismantling the legacy accounting model. He's not selling tax prep. He's building advisory-first relationships with business owners and high-net-worth individuals who are tired of generic service and reactive hand-holding.

He speaks from the trenches. He's building something different and he's willing to say what's broken about the industry.

## Tone & Personality

**What he sounds like:**
- Direct, honest, slightly uncomfortable (in a good way)
- Practical and grounded in real client situations
- Candid about what doesn't work and why
- Confident but not arrogant
- Speaking as a peer to peers (high-net-worth individuals, business owners, other advisors)

**What he does NOT sound like:**
- Generic CPA firm ("We provide quality service with integrity and professionalism")
- Cookie-cutter ("We do audit, bookkeeping, tax prep, tax planning, business valuations...")
- Social media tax hacks and gimmicks ("Five quick tips to save taxes")
- Oversimplified financial advice without context
- 1970s compliance-first ("We prepare your tax return")
- Trendy or performative ("Your financial journey awaits!")

**How he speaks:**
- Declarative sentences, no hedging
- First-person (I, we) not corporate speak
- Specific examples from real clients
- Acknowledges the uncomfortable truth
- Respects his audience's time and intelligence

## Writing Rules

1. **Lead with specificity.** Not "we serve business owners." Instead: "I work with business owners doing $5–20M in revenue who are tired of once-a-year tax prep."

2. **Avoid corporate jargon.** No "leverage," "optimize," "holistic," "solutions," "synergy," "proactive engagement." Speak plainly.

3. **Use metaphors from his actual experience.** The Maker's Mark/Bush prices charity metaphor. The hamster wheel of compliance. The optometrist case.

4. **Be direct about the tradeoff.** Some clients won't like the boundaries. That's okay. It's filtering, not a bug.

5. **Acknowledge the discomfort.** "This requires some discomfort, but it's necessary." He's not afraid to state hard truths.

6. **No disclaimers or hedging.** "I'm not tax advice, consult your advisor" is fine at the end if needed, but don't soften the main point with qualifiers.

7. **Keep paragraphs short.** LinkedIn rewards scanability. 2-3 sentences per thought block.

8. **One clear idea per post.** Don't try to cram three concepts into a single post.

---

## Formatting Standards

**Text Posts (8 total):**
- Hook in first line (question, statement, or surprising fact)
- 2-4 short body sections
- No more than 150-200 words
- Clear call-to-action or thought-provoker at the end
- Hashtags minimal (2-3 max, relevant)

**Graphic Posts (4 total):**
- Chart, diagram, or comparison visual
- Post copy is shorter (75-100 words max)
- Asset supports the narrative (don't just illustrate, amplify)
- Examples: side-by-side compliance vs. advisory, tax planning timeline, quarterly cadence, pricing transparency framework

---

## LinkedIn Best Practices Luke Should Follow

1. **Post Tuesday-Thursday for highest engagement** (except for his Monday kicks to establish rhythm)
2. **Pin the first/strongest post** for at least a week
3. **Reply to comments in the first 2 hours** (shows active engagement, signals to algorithm)
4. **Encourage conversation, not just consumption** (ask a real question, not "what are your thoughts?")
5. **Use line breaks and white space** (wall of text loses people)
6. **Link back to website or Calendly** only when it's genuinely relevant, not forced
